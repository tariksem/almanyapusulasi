Fixed-term jobs: Can contracts last 4 years? Not yet

The 30-second summary

If your employment contract has an end date, this proposal may matter to you. The government wants a change, but nothing changes automatically today.

Government bill · Not in force

What is changing?

Under the general current rule, a fixed-term contract without an objective reason can usually last up to two years, with up to three extensions. The cabinet's proposal would allow up to four years and six extensions for relevant hires through 31 December 2030.

Who is affected?

People starting a new job, employees on fixed-term contracts and employers. Different legal rules may apply where there is a valid objective reason for the contract.

Simple example

For example, if an employer offers you a three-year fixed-term contract without a reason, do not assume that this proposal already makes it lawful.

What should you do?

  • Check the expiry date and extension clauses in your existing contract.
  • If an employer says the new rule already applies, ask for the enacted law and effective date.
  • Seek qualified employment-law advice if you face a specific contract or dismissal problem.
Important: Legal status

As of 18 November 2026, this is a cabinet-approved draft law. Parliament may change it.
