Living in Germany: Practical Encyclopedia

Clear answers to the little questions that make everyday life easier. Housing, recycling, neighbours, shopping and much more in one place.

12 practical topics
Home & neighbourhood

How do I separate rubbish?

Yellow, paper, organic waste and residual waste explained.

Packaging usually belongs in the yellow bin or bag; clean paper and cardboard in the blue bin; suitable food and garden waste in the organic bin; and remaining household waste in residual waste. Separate glass packaging by colour at collection points. Return deposit bottles (Pfand). Local collection rules and dates vary.

What should I do next?Find your municipality's waste collection calendar (Abfallkalender) and save the collection dates.
Home & neighbourhood

How do I rent a home?

Listings, SCHUFA, deposits and handover records.

Compare Kaltmiete (base rent), Nebenkosten (service charges) and Warmmiete (rent plus estimated charges). Landlords may ask for income evidence or SCHUFA. Never transfer money before verifying the property and contract. Record the condition with photos in an Übergabeprotokoll at handover; check the legal deposit limit.

What should I do next?Prepare a rental application folder and compare the full monthly cost, including heating.
Social life

Getting along with neighbours

Quiet hours, house rules and introductions.

Read the Hausordnung and building notices. Avoid disruptive noise, especially at night; quiet hours depend on local rules and the building. A brief introduction and advance notice of unusual activity can prevent friction.

What should I do next?Introduce yourself and ask about shared facilities, bins and laundry rules.
Social life

How do I make friends in Germany?

Clubs, adult education, volunteering and neighbourhood events.

Sports clubs (Vereine), Volkshochschule (VHS) courses, volunteering, libraries and local meetups are practical ways to meet people. Regular participation tends to work better than one-off events.

What should I do next?Find a local club directory or VHS course and choose one activity you can attend every week.
Administration

How do I register my address?

Anmeldung, the landlord confirmation and Bürgeramt.

When you move into a new home in Germany, you generally must register the address within two weeks. Offices commonly require your ID or passport and a Wohnungsgeberbestätigung from the housing provider. Appointment and online options vary by municipality.

What should I do next?Check Bürgeramt appointments early and request your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung.
Healthcare

Doctor appointments and emergencies

When to call a Hausarzt, 116117 or 112.

Call 112 for life-threatening emergencies. For urgent but non-life-threatening medical issues outside office hours, call the medical on-call service at 116117. For routine health concerns, start with a GP (Hausarzt) and bring your insurance card.

What should I do next?Save 112 and 116117 and look up a GP near your home.
Families

First steps for families

Daycare, school enrolment and family benefits.

Daycare and school enrolment procedures vary by state and municipality. Apply early and keep vaccination and health records, proof of address and prior school records ready. Check eligibility for Kindergeld and other benefits separately.

What should I do next?Find your municipality's daycare portal and school enrolment guidance; record deadlines.
Students

Managing a student budget

Shared flats, semester fees and student transport.

When looking at WG shared flats, compare total costs and house rules. Semesterbeitrag is not automatically the same as tuition; provisions differ by institution. Check where your student ticket is valid and explore accommodation and advice from your Studierendenwerk.

What should I do next?Check your university's AStA and Studierendenwerk pages for housing, meals and transport.
Administration

Received a broadcasting fee letter?

How the household contribution works.

Rundfunkbeitrag is generally paid per dwelling, not per person. If someone in your household already pays, you may need their Beitragsnummer. Exemptions or reductions can apply in specific circumstances, but they are not automatic.

What should I do next?Verify the letter and use the official Rundfunkbeitrag website to respond or apply for an exemption.
Everyday life

Public transport without surprises

Ticket validity, subscriptions and delays.

Check fare zones, validity periods, transfers and whether a ticket is personal before travel. Subscription products such as Deutschlandticket have cancellation rules. Passenger rights for train delays depend on ticket and journey conditions.

What should I do next?Keep your ticket and ID accessible; save subscription renewal and cancellation dates.
Everyday life

Shopping and the Pfand system

Deposits, receipts and opening times.

Some drink containers carry a refundable deposit (Pfand). Return them at the machine and redeem the receipt at checkout. Most shops close on Sundays, with exceptions. Compare the Grundpreis (unit price) on labels.

What should I do next?Check Pfand labels and the unit price during your next grocery trip.
Everyday life

Avoiding online shopping scams

Fake shops, classifieds and safer payments.

Unusually low prices, missing legal contact details (Impressum) and bank transfer-only payment demands can be warning signs. Verify sellers, do not rely on listing photos alone and consider payment methods with buyer protection.

What should I do next?Before paying an unfamiliar seller, check the Impressum and independent reviews.
