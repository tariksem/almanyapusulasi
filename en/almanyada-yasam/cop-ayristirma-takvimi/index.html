Waste sorting and collection days in Germany

Which bin, which day, and what happens when waste is sorted incorrectly?

The basics

Yellow bins or bags usually take sales packaging; blue bins take paper and cardboard. Follow local rules for organic and residual waste, which differ by municipality.

How to proceed

Search your municipality or waste provider for Abfallkalender. Enter your address to see the collection timetable; some locations require bins to be placed at the kerb.

Important details

Return deposit bottles at Pfand machines. Take glass to colour-coded containers, and batteries and electrical devices to approved collection points. Find out how to book Sperrmüll collection for bulky items.

Mistakes and practical advice

Common mistakes include putting greasy cardboard into paper recycling, assuming every plastic object belongs in the yellow bin, or following a neighbour’s collection dates. Check local instructions.

Practical guide: Real-world steps

Where does it go? Everyday waste examples

  1. Empty yoghurt pots, drink cartons and packaging film generally go into the yellow bin or bag. Separate removable packaging components; emptying is usually enough without washing.

  2. Clean cardboard and paper go into the paper stream. Check local guidance before recycling food-soiled pizza boxes.

  3. Return deposit bottles through Pfand machines; sort non-deposit glass packaging by colour in Altglas containers. Take batteries and electronics to approved collection points.

  4. Enter your address into the municipal Abfallkalender. Check holiday changes and local requirements for putting bins out.

Frequently asked questions

Does all plastic go into yellow recycling?

No. The yellow stream is generally for packaging; some municipalities operate broader Wertstofftonne collection.

Can I throw glass away at night?

Local hours may restrict noisy glass-container use; follow posted signs.
