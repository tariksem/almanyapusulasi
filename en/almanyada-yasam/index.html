Waste & household · 01

Which bin? Think packaging, not just colour

First check whether an item is packaging, then confirm the local collection system.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Put clean, dry paper and cardboard into paper recycling.
  2. Use local yellow collection for empty lightweight packaging; sort non-returnable glass packaging into glass containers.
  3. Take batteries, electronics and leftover paint to designated collection points.

Watch out: The yellow bin is not for every plastic item. Municipal collection rules vary.

German word: Mülltrennung

Waste & household · 02

When should you put the bins out?

Don't guess; find the official collection calendar for your street and house.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Find the official municipal Abfuhrkalender or waste service.
  2. Choose your street, house number and waste type.
  3. Check public holiday changes and reminder options.

Watch out: A specific collection day cannot be confirmed without an address.

German word: Abfuhrkalender

Waste & household · 03

Old sofa, battery or phone: where does it go?

Bulky furniture, batteries and electronics have different collection routes.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Check municipal Sperrmüll services for bulky furniture.
  2. Take batteries and electronic devices to authorised return points.
  3. Check booking, quantity and fee rules before visiting a recycling centre.

Watch out: Don't leave bulky waste on the pavement without checking local rules.

German word: Sperrmüll / Wertstoffhof

Shopping & savings · 04

Pfand: why your bottle is worth money

Single-use deposits are usually 25 cents; reusable bottles often carry 8 or 15 cents.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Check the Einweg/Mehrweg label and deposit markings.
  2. Return the empty container at a suitable machine and collect the receipt.
  3. Redeem the receipt at the till or request the applicable refund.

Watch out: Not all bottles have a deposit; return rules depend on the type of container.

German word: Pfandautomat

Home & neighbours · 05

Getting along with neighbours: three small habits

Knowing the building's house rules can prevent many everyday conflicts.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Ask for the Hausordnung and shared-space rules.
  2. Give neighbours notice before planned noisy activity.
  3. Start with a calm, specific conversation if something goes wrong.

Watch out: There is no single nationwide timetable for every noise rule; check local requirements.

German word: Hausordnung / Ruhezeiten

Home & neighbours · 06

Parcel delivered to a neighbour?

Tracking information and a delivery note are the first clues.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Check the carrier's tracking record.
  2. Look for a neighbour's name or collection point on the notice.
  3. If missing, contact the carrier and the sender.

Watch out: A 'delivered' status does not always mean you received it personally.

German word: Nachbar / Abholstation

Social life · 07

Making friends: start with regular encounters

Shared activities you attend repeatedly can make connecting easier.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Try a club, VHS course or volunteering group.
  2. Ask when the next meeting is and go again.
  3. Suggest a simple follow-up: 'Nächste Woche wieder?'

Watch out: Friendships take time; don't expect instant closeness.

German word: Verein / Ehrenamt

Singles & WG · 08

Before renting a WG room, ask these questions

Rent is only one part of shared-flat life; clarify terms and expectations.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Ask what rent includes: utilities, internet and electricity.
  2. Check the main lease, sublease and deposit conditions.
  3. Discuss cleaning, visitors, smoking and quiet times.

Watch out: Don't pay in advance for a room you have not seen.

German word: WG / Untermietvertrag

Home & neighbours · 09

Seven red flags in rental listings

Low prices, urgent payments and 'keys by courier' can indicate fraud.

Show practical steps ⌄What to do
  1. Check the listing photos, address and advertiser.
  2. Don't pay for a flat you have not viewed and verified.
  3. Keep messages and report suspicious activity to the platform and police.

Watch out: A major listing site is not a guarantee of authenticity.
