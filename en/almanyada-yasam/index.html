Living in Germany Encyclopedia

From rubbish collection to making friends: practical answers for everyday life in Germany.

Home and daily life

Waste sorting and collection days

Yellow bins or bags generally take packaging; blue bins are for paper and cardboard. Organic and residual waste rules vary locally.

Practical guide

Check your municipality's Abfallkalender for collection days. Separate glass by colour and return deposit bottles at reverse-vending machines.

When moving in, ask about communal bins and the local bulky-waste (Sperrmüll) collection system.

Home and daily life

Finding a rental and avoiding scams

Landlords may ask for income evidence, a SCHUFA report or a short introduction before a viewing.

Practical guide

Never transfer money before verifying the property, tenancy agreement and handover arrangements. Compare cold rent, ancillary charges and heating costs separately.

Ask for a Wohnungsgeberbestätigung and register your new address within the applicable deadline.

Social life

Neighbours, quiet hours and building etiquette

The building's Hausordnung may set quiet hours, stairwell cleaning and rules for shared spaces; local rules vary.

Practical guide

Introduce yourself briefly and respect shared areas. A polite conversation can resolve noise issues before they escalate.

Check local restrictions before noisy renovation work on Sundays and public holidays.

Social life

Making friends and building a social circle

Sports clubs (Vereine), adult education classes, volunteering and neighbourhood events are good places to meet people regularly.

Practical guide

Attend the same group over several weeks rather than only once. International groups can help while you improve your German.

Look up your city's events calendar and volunteering opportunities.

Family and health

Starting daycare and school

Many cities manage daycare applications through local portals. Deadlines and allocation criteria vary; school procedures depend on the federal state.

Practical guide

Follow parent meetings, school messages and emergency contact requests. Ask separately about lunches, excursions and after-school care.

Start early and rely on municipal and school announcements for deadlines.

Family and health

Doctors, appointments and emergencies

Your GP (Hausarzt) is often the first point of contact. For urgent but non-life-threatening medical assistance outside regular hours, call 116117.

Practical guide

Call 112 for life-threatening emergencies. Bring your insurance card, medication list and relevant medical records to appointments.

116117 and 112 serve different situations; choose the appropriate service.

Students

Student budgets, travel and discounts

Check your university's semester contribution, transport entitlements, canteen and library. Semester ticket validity can differ.

Practical guide

When looking for a shared flat (WG), review deposit, service charges and notice period before signing.

Your local Studierendenwerk can advise on accommodation and student support.

New arrivals

Address registration and first documents

As a general rule, register your new address within two weeks of moving in; appointments and documentation depend on the municipality.

Practical guide

Usually you need identification and a Wohnungsgeberbestätigung. Registration is separate from residence permits and insurance.

Check your Bürgeramt's official page for the required documents.

Home and daily life

Broadcasting contribution per dwelling

Germany's broadcasting contribution is generally assessed per dwelling, not individually for every resident.

Practical guide

In shared accommodation, clarify who pays. Exemptions or reductions depend on your circumstances.

Do not ignore letters; use the official rundfunkbeitrag.de website.

New arrivals

Phone, internet and subscription contracts

Read minimum terms, renewals and cancellation rules for mobile, broadband, gym and energy contracts.

Practical guide

Keep the written contract summary and price terms. Moving house does not automatically end every contract.

Check the notice period (Kündigungsfrist) and any special cancellation rights.
